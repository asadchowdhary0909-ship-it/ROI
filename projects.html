🔭 DIY TELESCOPE GUIDE

Build a simple 70 mm / 700 mm refractor telescope using cardboard and basic craft materials. Follow all seven steps carefully, check alignment at every stage, and test the telescope during daylight before observing the night sky.

⚠️ SAFETY FIRST

NEVER point this telescope at the Sun. Looking at the Sun through an unfiltered telescope can permanently damage your eyes.

Use the cutter and hot glue gun carefully and, if necessary, have an adult help with cutting, gluing and mounting.

1

STEP 1 — EXACT CARDBOARD CUTTING PLAN

First build the main telescope tube. The tube must be straight, strong and long enough for the 700 mm optical system.

📏 Main Tube Panels

Panel length 700 mm
Panel width 82 mm
Number of panels 4
Tube type Square cardboard tube
Cut four identical 700 × 82 mm cardboard panels.

⭕ Objective Rings

Outside diameter 82 mm
Inside diameter 70 mm
Purpose Hold objective lens

🖤 Internal Baffles

Cut three circular baffles. These reduce stray light entering the optical path.

Baffle outside diameter 76 mm
Baffle opening 60 mm
Quantity 3
Approx. positions 200 / 400 / 550 mm
TIP: Paint the inside of the tube matte black. A dark interior helps reduce unwanted reflections.

✂️ Cutting Sequence

  1. Measure the first 700 × 82 mm panel.
  2. Cut it carefully.
  3. Use it as a template for the remaining three panels.
  4. Draw the 82 mm outer circles for the objective rings.
  5. Cut the 70 mm inner openings.
  6. Cut the three 76/60 mm baffles.
  7. Mark the baffle positions inside the tube.
  8. Dry-fit everything before using glue.
2

STEP 2 — 70 mm OBJECTIVE HOLDER + ADJUSTABLE FOCUSER

The objective lens is the main light-gathering element. Our design uses a 70 mm objective with a 700 mm focal length.

Objective at the front, cardboard optical tube in the middle, adjustable focuser at the rear.

⭕ Objective Holder

Objective 70 mm
Focal length 700 mm
Ring outer diameter 82 mm
Ring inner diameter 70 mm
Holder retaining ID ≈72 mm
Front sleeve 250 × 90 mm

🔧 Adjustable Focuser

Focuser collar 90 × 90 mm
Sliding section 500 × 70 mm
Eyepiece housing 60 × 45 mm
Eyepiece opening ≈32 mm
Adjustment travel 50–100 mm

🛠️ Build the Focuser

  1. Make the fixed rear collar from cardboard.
  2. Make the sliding inner tube slightly smaller so it can move smoothly.
  3. Keep the sliding section straight.
  4. Add thin cardboard strips if the fit is too loose.
  5. Add the approximately 32 mm eyepiece holder.
  6. Test the full 50–100 mm focusing movement before permanently gluing the parts.
The focuser should move smoothly but should not wobble. A loose focuser makes accurate focusing difficult.
3

STEP 3 — OPTICAL ALIGNMENT & DAYLIGHT TESTING

Before taking the telescope outside at night, make sure the optical parts are centered and the tube is straight.

The objective, baffles and eyepiece should share the same optical centerline.

✅ Alignment Checklist

☐ Objective is centered in its holder.
☐ Objective is not tilted.
☐ Main tube is straight.
☐ Baffles are centered.
☐ Focuser is aligned with the tube.
☐ Eyepiece sits straight.

🌳 Daylight Test

  1. Point the telescope at a very distant object.
  2. Start with the 25 mm eyepiece.
  3. Slowly move the focuser.
  4. Find the sharpest image.
  5. Check whether the image is centered.
  6. Check whether the tube or focuser wobbles.

🔬 Magnification

Eyepiece Calculation Magnification
25 mm 700 ÷ 25 28×
10 mm 700 ÷ 10 70×
Always begin with the lower magnification eyepiece. It gives a wider field of view and makes objects easier to find.
4

STEP 4 — CARDBOARD ALT-AZIMUTH MOUNT

The telescope needs a stable mount. An altitude-azimuth mount allows the telescope to move up/down and left/right.

The altitude axis controls up/down movement; the azimuth base controls left/right movement.

🛠️ Build the Mount

  1. Make two strong cardboard side supports.
  2. Create an altitude pivot on both sides of the tube.
  3. Make a circular or square azimuth base.
  4. Attach the altitude supports to the base.
  5. Build three strong folded triangular legs.
  6. Reinforce all joints with extra cardboard layers.
  7. Place the telescope near its balance point.
  8. Test movement before adding the final glue.
The mount should move smoothly but should not be so loose that the telescope drops or shakes.

⚖️ Balance Test

Place the telescope in the mount and slowly move it up and down. If the front or rear suddenly falls, move the pivot closer to the telescope's balance point.

5

STEP 5 — FINDER & ALIGNMENT

A finder helps you aim the main telescope. Instead of searching through the narrow high-magnification view, use the finder to point toward the target first.

Simple 150 × 35 × 35 mm cardboard finder with a center crosshair.

📏 Finder Dimensions

Length 150 mm
Width 35 mm
Height 35 mm

➕ Make the Crosshair

  1. Make the finder body.
  2. Cut or draw a small viewing opening.
  3. Draw a vertical line.
  4. Draw a horizontal line crossing it.
  5. The intersection becomes the aiming point.

🎯 Align the Finder

  1. During daylight, point the main telescope at a distant stationary object.
  2. Center the object in the 25 mm eyepiece.
  3. Without moving the main telescope, look through the finder.
  4. Adjust the finder until the same object sits at the crosshair.
  5. Repeat until the main telescope and finder agree.
Align the finder during daylight. It makes nighttime target finding much easier.
6

STEP 6 — PHONE ADAPTER + FINAL BUILD

Once the telescope works visually, add a simple cardboard smartphone adapter so you can experiment with photographing what you see through the eyepiece.

Keep the phone camera centered over the eyepiece opening.

📱 Phone Platform

Example phone size 160 × 75 mm
Example platform 180 × 95 mm
Eyepiece opening 32–33 mm
Phone clamp 40–50 mm

🛠️ Build the Phone Adapter

  1. Measure your phone.
  2. Make a platform slightly larger than the phone.
  3. Cut a centered 32–33 mm opening for the eyepiece.
  4. Add side supports to stop the phone sliding.
  5. Add a 40–50 mm clamp/support.
  6. Center the phone camera over the eyepiece.
  7. Test the adapter without pointing at the Sun.

🎨 Final Telescope Finishing

The telescope should be mechanically finished before you begin serious observing.
7

STEP 7 — FIRST NIGHT ASTRONOMY GUIDE

Your telescope is now ready for its first observing session. Start slowly and learn how the telescope moves, focuses and tracks objects.

Begin with a low-power eyepiece and slowly move toward higher magnification.

🌙 First Target — The Moon

  1. Set the telescope on stable ground.
  2. Make sure the mount moves smoothly.
  3. Install the 25 mm eyepiece.
  4. Use the finder to aim at the Moon.
  5. Look through the eyepiece.
  6. Slowly adjust the focuser.
  7. Center the Moon.
  8. If the image is stable, try the 10 mm eyepiece.

🔭 Magnification Guide

Eyepiece Magnification Use
25 mm 28× Finding and centering objects
10 mm 70× Higher-detail observation

🪐 Planet Observing

After mastering the Moon, you can try bright planets when they are visible in the sky. Start with the 25 mm eyepiece and center the target before increasing magnification.

Jupiter can show a bright planetary disk and, under suitable conditions, its bright moons. Saturn can show its distinctive ring system when it is positioned well for observation.

📱 Using the Phone

  1. Center the object visually first.
  2. Attach the phone adapter.
  3. Keep the phone camera centered over the eyepiece.
  4. Use the phone screen to focus carefully.
  5. Keep the telescope steady.
  6. Take several photographs rather than relying on one.

🌌 First Night Checklist

☐ Telescope assembled.
☐ Objective secure.
☐ Focuser moves smoothly.
☐ Finder aligned.
☐ Mount stable.
☐ 25 mm eyepiece installed first.
☐ Target centered.
☐ Focus adjusted.
☐ 10 mm eyepiece tested only after centering.
☐ Phone adapter securely attached.
☀️ NEVER LOOK AT THE SUN THROUGH THIS TELESCOPE. Do not attempt solar observation unless a proper, telescope-specific solar filter is correctly installed at the front of the optical system.
